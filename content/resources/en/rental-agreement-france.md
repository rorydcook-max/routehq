French law requires consumer contracts in French ([Loi Toubon, Article 2](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/loda/id/JORFTEXT000000349929)), so this version puts French first, with English beside it, and the renter-friendly reading where they differ.

## What the France version covers

**Camera fines.** A company that owns a vehicle must [designate the driver within 45 days](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006074228/) for offenses caught by automatic camera, or pay a heavier fine itself. The agreement says you will designate the renter.

**Disputes.** Every business selling to consumers must offer a free consumer mediator (Consumer Code L612-1), and the consumer can sue at home ([Consumer Code](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006069565/)).

**Two wheels.** Helmets and approved gloves for rider and passenger; a B license held two years plus 7 hours' training allows 125 cc in France.

**Zones and seasons.** Crit'Air stickers for low-emission zones, and winter tires or chains in mountain areas from 1 November to 31 March.

Every EU version also follows EU consumer law: prices shown with every compulsory charge included, damage charged only at its evidenced cost and never above the excess when a damage waiver applies, no keeping the whole deposit as a penalty, and the [14-day withdrawal right not applying to car rental for set dates](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj). Fines and renter details follow the GDPR, and the renter signs on paper or electronically, both valid under [eIDAS](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj).
