German courts read rental terms as standard business terms ([BGB sections 305 to 310](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__305.html)): unclear or surprising clauses and flat-rate damages higher than the real loss don't hold. This version keeps charges to actual, evidenced costs and lets the renter prove a lower loss.

## What the Germany version covers

**Fines.** When the authorities send a hearing letter for an offense during the rental, you name the renter as the driver; parking costs charged to you as keeper ([StVG section 25a](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvg/__25a.html)) are passed on.

**Licenses.** Visitors from outside the EU can drive for six months, with a translation where needed ([FeV section 29](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/fev_2010/__29.html)); B196 allows 125 cc for drivers over 25.

**Winter.** In wintry conditions only winter or all-season tires with the Alpine symbol are allowed ([StVO section 2(3a)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__2.html)).

**Zones.** Umweltzonen need the green sticker, which you provide.

Every EU version also follows EU consumer law: prices shown with every compulsory charge included, damage charged only at its evidenced cost and never above the excess when a damage waiver applies, no keeping the whole deposit as a penalty, and the [14-day withdrawal right not applying to car rental for set dates](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj). Fines and renter details follow the GDPR, and the renter signs on paper or electronically, both valid under [eIDAS](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj).
