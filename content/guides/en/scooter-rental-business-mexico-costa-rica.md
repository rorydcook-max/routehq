Beach towns on Mexico's Caribbean and Pacific coasts and Costa Rica's surf and nature towns have the same rental pattern as Southeast Asia's islands: visitors arrive without a car, stay a week or a month, and want a scooter or motorbike to get around. Starting a small rental shop there follows the same logic, with each country's own registration and insurance rules.

> This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules differ by state, province and municipality and change over time. Sources were checked on 8 November 2026. Confirm the details locally before you buy bikes.

## Customers and fleet

Your customers are mostly tourists renting for a few days to a week, plus long-stay visitors, remote workers and seasonal staff renting by the month. Choose bikes that suit them:

- **110–125 cc automatic scooters** for most renters: cheap to buy, easy to ride, easy to repair.
- **150–160 cc scooters** for couples, longer rides and hills, at a higher rate.
- **Dual-sport or trail bikes** where roads are rough or unpaved, for experienced riders only, with a bigger deposit.

Stick to one or two common brands so parts and mechanics are easy to find, and start with five to ten bikes. Our general guide on [starting a scooter rental business](/guides/start-a-scooter-rental-business) covers fleet, maintenance and break-even in detail.

## Mexico

### Registration and tax

Register with the tax authority (SAT) for an RFC tax number and issue electronic invoices (CFDI) for your rentals; an accountant (contador) will set up the right tax regime ([SAT](https://www.sat.gob.mx), accessed 8 November 2026). Your municipality will also have its own business operating license and land-use rules for your shop or yard. Each bike needs current state registration and plates.

### Compulsory liability insurance

Article 63 Bis of the Federal Roads, Bridges and Motor Transport Law requires every vehicle that travels on federal roads and bridges to carry insurance that covers damage to third parties ([Ley de Caminos, Puentes y Autotransporte Federal](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LCPAF.pdf), accessed 8 November 2026). The financial consumer protection agency CONDUSEF notes that Mexico City's traffic regulations also require insurance, so federal-road cover alone may not be enough everywhere ([CONDUSEF](https://www.condusef.gob.mx/?p=contenido&idc=630&idcat=1), accessed 8 November 2026).

For a rental business, buy a policy written for rental use that covers liability for any authorized renter, and ask whether damage and theft cover is worth it for your bikes. Check your state's rules too.

### Renters and licenses

Rules on which licenses foreign visitors may ride on vary by state. At minimum, check that every renter holds a valid license that covers motorcycles, and record its details on the agreement. Insurance may refuse a claim if the rider wasn't licensed for the bike.

## Costa Rica

### Registration and the tourism declaration

Register your business and tax status with the Ministry of Finance (Hacienda) and get your municipal commercial license (patente) for the shop. The Costa Rican Tourism Board (ICT) offers a tourism declaration, which is voluntary. Vehicle rental companies are one of the listed business types, and the ICT lists benefits such as inclusion in its official listings, promotion on its website and participation in tourism fairs; it says declarations do not grant tax exemptions of any kind ([ICT: tourism declaration](https://www.ict.go.cr/es/servicios-institucionales/declaratoria-turistica.html), accessed 8 November 2026). The ICT also publishes [specific rules for vehicle rental businesses that hold a declaration](https://www.ict.go.cr/es/documentos-institucionales/legislaci%C3%B3n-de-empresas/leyes-y-reglamentos/607-disposiciones-reguladoras-para-la-actividad-de-las-empresas-de-arrendamiento-de-vehiculos-a-turistas-nacionales-y-extranjeros.html) (accessed 8 November 2026).

### Compulsory insurance with the marchamo

Every vehicle pays the marchamo, the annual road-use charge, and the compulsory motor insurance (Seguro Obligatorio Automotor, SOA) is acquired automatically when you pay it. The national insurer INS says the SOA covers injury, disability and death in traffic accidents, including medical and rehabilitation care ([INS: marchamo and SOA](https://www.grupoins.com/seguros-personas/marchamo/seguro-obligatorio-automotor-marchamo/), accessed 8 November 2026). It doesn't cover damage to the bike or to other people's property, so decide how you'll cover those: voluntary insurance, a deposit, or both.

Put every bike's marchamo date in a calendar. A bike without a current marchamo shouldn't leave the shop.

### Tourists and foreign licenses

Article 91 of Costa Rica's traffic law (Law 9078) lets drivers with a foreign license who are in the country as tourists drive the same type of vehicle their license allows for three months ([Ley 9078, Article 91, SINALEVI](https://sinalevi.go.cr/ResultadosNormativa/Informacion?param1=73504&param2=90232&param3=3&param4=92), accessed 8 November 2026). The key words are "the same type of vehicle": a renter with a car-only license isn't licensed for a motorbike. Check the license category, check the entry stamp date for long-stay renters, and record both.

## Helmets and safety

Supply certified helmets in several sizes for every rider and passenger, with liners or hairnets, and replace any helmet that has been dropped hard or involved in a crash. Add a phone holder, a rain poncho for wet season, and a card with your number and what to do after an accident.

## Pricing

Set daily, weekly and monthly prices for each model. Check what nearby shops charge for the same bikes, then work out your cost per bike per day: purchase price over its working life, insurance, registration, servicing, tires, parking and a damage reserve. Discount for longer rentals, but make sure each rate still covers your costs. Our free [pricing calculator](/resources/pricing-calculator) does the sums for you.

## Deposits, agreements and photos

- **Deposit:** size it to the bike's value and your insurance excess. Avoid holding passports: travelers need them, and a cash, transfer or card deposit backed by a signed agreement protects you better. See [how much deposit to charge](/guides/rental-deposit-policy).
- **Agreement:** every rental, even one day, in English and Spanish. Start from our free [rental agreement](/resources/rental-agreement).
- **Photos:** photograph each bike at handover and return, with the renter present, from the same angles every time. Use our [handover checklist](/guides/vehicle-handover-checklist).
