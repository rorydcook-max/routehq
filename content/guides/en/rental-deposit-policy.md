*By Anan · Updated 28 October 2026*

The deposit is where most rental arguments start. Too low and it does not cover a broken mirror. Too high and you lose bookings to the shop next door. Taken the wrong way, like holding a passport, it can land you in legal trouble.

This guide covers how much to charge, how to take it, what you can deduct, when to refund, and how to handle the disputes that will happen sooner or later.

> This guide is general information, not legal advice. Rules on deposits and identity documents differ by country, so check with your local office if you are unsure.

## Typical deposit sizes by vehicle class

There is no official rate. The ranges below come from public operator listings we found, so treat them as a sense of the market, not a rule. Check what shops near you ask for, then adjust for your bikes and cars.

| Country | Scooter 110–125cc | Larger scooter / big bike | Car | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thailand (THB) | ฿3,000 cash is common | ฿5,000–15,000 | ฿5,000–10,000 for 1.2–1.6L; ฿15,000–20,000 for 2.0L and above | [Phuket 101](https://www.phuket101.net/renting-a-motorbike-in-phuket/), [Drive Car Rental](https://business.drivecarrental.com/terms-conditions) |
| Indonesia, Bali (IDR) | Often a passport copy plus a cash deposit; amounts vary | Higher, set by bike value | IDR 250,000–1,000,000 on self-drive listings | [Pelago listing](https://pelago.com/en/activity/pg57z-self-drive-car-rental-bali-bali) |
| Vietnam (VND/USD) | VND 500,000–800,000 in Da Nang | Card deposits around USD 500 for monthly rentals in Hanoi | Varies widely | [Vinpearl](https://vinpearl.com/en/da-nang-motorbike-rental-the-best-guide-with-updated-prices), [Tigit Motorbikes](https://www.tigitmotorbikes.com/?p=15710) |
| Philippines (PHP) | ₱1,000–2,000 in Siargao | Higher, set by bike value | ₱5,000 for self-drive | [Klook listing](https://www.klook.com/en-PH/activity/83890-siargao-motorbike-rental/), [Ahround](https://ahroundph.com/car-rental-reservation) |
| Malaysia (MYR) | RM100 in Langkawi | Higher, set by bike value | RM200 sedan; RM300 MPV, van or luxury | [Headout listing](https://www.headout.com/carmotorbike-rental-in-langkawi/motorbike-rental-langkawi-e-19465/) |

A simple way to set your own figure:

1. **List your five most common damage repairs** for that model (mirror, side panel, brake lever, scratched body, lost key) and add up a realistic bad case.
2. **Compare with one to three weeks of rent.** If the repair total is far above that, take a bigger deposit or offer optional damage cover.
3. **Check the market.** If you are much higher than nearby shops, explain why on your booking page (newer bikes, delivery, fast refunds).
4. **Scale for long rentals.** A monthly renter has the bike much longer. Many operators take a deposit closer to one month's rent.

## Offering a lower deposit with damage cover

Some renters, especially those who are short of cash at the start of a trip, will pay a little more per day to leave a smaller deposit. Some shops in Phuket, for example, waive the deposit if the renter buys the shop's optional cover ([Phuket 101](https://www.phuket101.net/renting-a-motorbike-in-phuket/)).

If you offer this, keep it simple and honest:

- **Charge a fixed daily fee** (for example ฿50 a day on a 125cc scooter) and state exactly what it covers, such as cosmetic damage up to a set amount.
- **Keep a small deposit** for keys, helmets and fuel, which the cover usually excludes.
- **Say clearly that it is not insurance** unless it is a real policy from a licensed insurer. Calling your own scheme "insurance" can mislead renters and, in some countries, breach insurance rules.
- **Exclude what you must:** riding without a licence, drink-riding, and taking the vehicle off the permitted area.

Track how much you collect against how much you pay out in repairs. If repairs regularly exceed the fees, raise the fee or narrow the cover.

## How to take the deposit

| Method | Good points | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | Instant, no fees, easy to refund | Keep it separate from takings, count it in front of the renter and write the amount on the agreement |
| **Bank transfer or national QR** (PromptPay, QRIS, VietQR, QR Ph, DuitNow) | No cash to store; a clear record | Check the money has actually arrived before handover. Fake slips are common, so match each slip to your bank statement |
| **Card pre-authorisation** | Nothing is charged unless you need it; tourists like it | Needs a card terminal or payment provider that supports holds. Holds expire, and releases can take up to two weeks to show on the renter's statement |
| **Card charge then refund** | Works with any card terminal | Card fees are usually not returned on refund; refunds take days |

Whatever you use, write the deposit amount, method and refund conditions on the agreement and get it signed before the keys leave your hand. RoveHQ reads payment slips and matches them to bookings, which helps with the fake slip problem.

## Why holding passports is a bad idea

Many shops still keep the renter's passport. It feels safe because nobody leaves the country without it. In practice it creates more problems than it solves.

- **It is restricted by law in several countries.** In Malaysia, a company CEO was charged under Section 12(1)(f) of the Passports Act 1966 for wrongfully holding passports belonging to others ([Malaysiakini](https://www.malaysiakini.com/news/506257)). In Vietnam, pledging or accepting a passport as a pledge can bring a fine of VND 3–5 million under Decree 144/2021, Article 18 ([Luat Duong Gia](https://luatduonggia.vn/phap-luat/ho-chieu-co-duoc-cam-co-khong-cam-duoc-bao-nhieu-tien/)). The Philippine passport law (Republic Act 11983) says a Philippine passport may not be confiscated by anyone other than the DFA, and using it as collateral is against the law ([DFA](https://jakartape.dfa.gov.ph/consular-services/passport/general-information-about-passports)).
- **Passports belong to the issuing government,** not the holder. Renters may need theirs for hotel check-in, immigration or a bank.
- **You become responsible for it.** If it is lost, stolen or damaged in your shop, you have a serious problem.
- **It does not pay for repairs.** You still have to negotiate money for damage, now with someone who is angry.

In Thailand and other countries where we could not find a specific rule, the safe approach is the same: take a money deposit, record the passport number and licence on the agreement, and take a photo of the ID page with the renter's consent. If you are unsure, check with your local police or tourist police.

## Deduction rules and evidence

Write your deduction rules into the agreement before the rental starts. A renter who agreed to a clear rule in advance rarely argues as hard afterwards.

Allow deductions only for:

- **New damage,** proven by comparing handover and return photos taken from the same angles.
- **Missing items:** keys, helmets, phone holders, documents.
- **Fuel shortfall,** if the agreement says the bike or car must come back at the same level.
- **Late return,** at an hourly or daily rate stated in the agreement.
- **Extra cleaning,** beyond normal sand and dust (for example, vomit or smoking in a car).
- **Traffic fines** from the rental period, with a copy of the ticket.

What makes a deduction stick:

- Handover photos with a timestamp, taken in front of the renter. Our [vehicle handover checklist guide](/guides/vehicle-handover-checklist) lists the 12 photos to take.
- A damage price list that the renter saw before signing.
- A repair quote or receipt for anything not on the list.
- An itemised breakdown given to the renter in writing.

Never deduct for normal wear: tyre wear, small stone chips on the front of a car or faded plastics.

## Writing a damage price list

A price list turns an argument into a lookup. Keep it to one page per vehicle type, use real prices from your mechanic or parts supplier, and review it every year.

**Example price list for a 125cc automatic scooter (illustrative prices only):**

| Item | Price |
|---|---|
| Mirror (each) | ฿150 |
| Brake lever (each) | ฿200 |
| Front side panel, scratched (respray) | ฿500 |
| Front side panel, cracked (replace) | ฿1,200 |
| Seat cover torn | ฿600 |
| Lost key (cut and program) | ฿500 |
| Helmet lost or damaged | ฿600 |
| Phone holder lost | ฿250 |
| Tow from within 10 km | ฿500 |

For anything not on the list, quote from your mechanic, share the quote with the renter and take the agreed amount. Attach the price list to every agreement. Our free bilingual [rental agreement](/resources/rental-agreement) has a section for it.

## Refund timing

Fast refunds win reviews. Slow ones lose them.

- **No damage found:** refund in full at the return inspection, in the same method it was paid.
- **Damage found:** agree the deduction on the spot if you can, using the price list. Refund the rest immediately.
- **Waiting for a repair quote:** tell the renter how long (for example, 48 hours), send the quote with photos, then refund the balance the same day you agree.
- **Possible traffic camera fines:** some operators hold back a small part of the deposit for a fixed number of days. Only do this if it is written in the agreement, and say the exact amount and date.
- **Card pre-authorisations:** release them as soon as the return is checked. Tell the renter their bank may take some days to show the release.

Write your refund timing on the agreement and your booking page.

## Handling disputes calmly

Some disputes will happen however careful you are. How you handle them decides whether you keep your reputation.

1. **Stay calm and move to the evidence.** Show the renter the handover photo and the return photo side by side. Most disputes end here.
2. **Point to the agreement and price list** they signed. Keep your tone factual, not accusing.
3. **Offer a fair middle ground** when the evidence is unclear, such as a scratch that may have been missed at handover. Losing ฿300 is cheaper than a one-star review.
4. **Put the outcome in writing,** in chat, with the amount deducted and the amount refunded.
5. **Do not keep the whole deposit as punishment.** Deduct only what the damage costs.

If the renter leaves a bad review:

- **Reply once, politely and briefly.** Thank them, state the facts without personal details, and say what you did. For example: "We refunded ฿2,500 of the ฿3,000 deposit after the return inspection. The ฿500 covered a replacement mirror, shown in the handover and return photos we shared with you."
- **Do not argue in the comments.** Future customers read your reply more than the complaint.
- **Learn from it.** If the same complaint keeps appearing, change the process, the price list or how you explain the deposit at handover.
