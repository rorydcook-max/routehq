Monthly rental sits between a weekly tourist rental and a lease. Renters get a car without a long contract or a credit check for finance; you get a vehicle that's rented for 30 days with one handover instead of five. Done well, it's the steadiest income a small rental business can have, and it fills the months when tourist demand drops.

> This guide is general information, not legal or tax advice. EU law sets the framework, and national tax, registration and consumer rules sit on top. Sources were checked on 8 November 2026.

## Who rents by the month

- **Remote workers and people relocating** who need a car for one to six months while they settle.
- **Seasonal and project workers:** hospitality staff in summer, construction and engineering crews, healthcare contracts.
- **Locals between cars,** waiting for a new car, a repair or an insurance settlement.
- **Students and researchers** on a semester or a fixed-term post.
- **Businesses** that need an extra vehicle for a few months without leasing.

They compare total monthly cost, flexibility to extend or end early, and how quickly problems get fixed. They don't need a premium car; they need a reliable one.

## Pricing a month

A monthly rate is usually far below thirty times your daily rate, but it has to cover a full month of costs: depreciation or finance, insurance, road tax, servicing, tires, cleaning between renters, a reserve for damage, and the days the car sits empty between rentals. Work out your cost per car per month first, then set the price. Our free [pricing calculator](/resources/pricing-calculator) does the sums.

Decide what's included up front: mileage allowance per month (and the price per extra kilometer), servicing and tires, roadside assistance, a replacement car if yours breaks down, and whether the renter can take the car abroad.

## VAT: the 30-day line

The EU VAT Directive has a special rule for hiring out means of transport. Article 56 says a short-term hire is taxed where the vehicle is actually put at the customer's disposal, and a hire that isn't short-term, to a customer who isn't a business, is taxed where the customer is established or usually lives. "Short-term" means continuous possession or use for no more than 30 days ([Council Directive 2006/112/EC, Article 56](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2006/112/oj/eng), accessed 8 November 2026).

What that means in practice:

- **Renters who live in your country:** charge your local VAT as usual, whatever the length.
- **Renters from another EU country on rentals over 30 days:** the place of supply can move to their country, which can mean charging that country's VAT and declaring it there, for example through the EU's One-Stop Shop. Talk to your accountant before you take these bookings, and keep proof of where each long-term renter lives.
- **Business renters:** different rules apply to business customers; your accountant will tell you how to invoice them.

## Insurance for long rentals

Third-party liability cover is compulsory for every vehicle in the EU under the Motor Insurance Directive ([Directive 2009/103/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/103/oj/eng), accessed 8 November 2026). For monthly renters, check three more things with your insurer:

- **Driver rules over long periods:** some policies limit how long one renter can keep a car, or need named drivers for rentals over a set length.
- **Use abroad:** long-term renters travel. Say in the agreement where the car may go, and confirm your policy and green card cover those countries.
- **Excess and damage:** a month of daily driving means more small damage. Decide how you'll handle scratches and wear at return, and put it in writing.

## Renters from other countries: registration rules

EU rules on vehicle registration are aimed at residents. The EU's citizen portal says you should not lend or rent a car registered in one EU country to a resident of another EU country for use there, because that person could be fined ([Your Europe: car registration in another EU country](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/registration/registration-abroad/index_en.htm), accessed 8 November 2026).

The normal monthly rental, a car registered in your country used here by a visitor or a local, is fine. Be careful when a renter wants to take your car home to another EU country for months, or when someone living in a neighboring country rents from you to use it there. Ask where the car will mostly be used, and record it.

## Deposits for long rentals

For a weekly rental, a card hold is common. For a monthly one, it often isn't practical: card holds expire after a limited time set by the card scheme and your payment provider, and under EU payment rules a hold needs the renter's consent to the exact amount ([Directive (EU) 2015/2366, Article 75](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2366/oj/eng), accessed 8 November 2026).

Most long-term operators take a deposit by bank transfer before handover, hold it on record, and return it after the final inspection, minus documented costs. Write down how long you take to return it and what you can deduct. Our [deposit guide](/guides/rental-deposit-policy) compares the options.

## Billing month by month

A simple pattern that renters understand:

1. **Before handover:** first month's rent plus the deposit.
2. **Each month:** rent due in advance on the same date, with a reminder a few days before.
3. **Grace period:** a short, written grace period, then a late fee or the right to recover the car, as your agreement and local law allow.
4. **Ending:** a notice period (for example, seven days), a final inspection with photos, then the deposit returned.

Tolls, parking and speeding tickets often arrive weeks after the event. Say in the agreement how you'll pass them on, including after the rental ends.

## The agreement

A monthly agreement needs everything a short one does, plus the rules for a rental that rolls on:

- Monthly rate, billing date, payment methods and what happens if a payment is late.
- Mileage allowance and excess-kilometer price.
- Who books and pays for servicing, and how often the renter must bring the car in.
- Notice to end, early return, and extensions.
- Where the car may be driven, including other countries.
- How damage and normal wear are told apart at return.

EU consumer rules on unfair terms and cancellation apply as they do to short rentals: see our guide to [EU rental agreements](/guides/rental-agreement-eu-consumer-law). Start from the free [rental agreement](/resources/rental-agreement), which has an open-ended monthly option.
