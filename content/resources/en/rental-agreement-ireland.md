Ireland's consumer regulator, the CCPC, is clear that a hire firm [may charge for repairs only where there is clear evidence the damage happened during the rental](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2026/06/16/consumer-rights-explained--i-think-my-holiday-car-hire-firm-is-trying-to-rip-me-off-what-are-my-rights). This agreement is built around that: a dated photo check at handover and return, written damage charges with the quote or invoice, and a second quote on request.

## What the Ireland version covers

**Licences.** EU, EEA and UK licences are valid; other visitors can drive on their own licence for up to 12 months. Learner permits are not accepted.

**Tolls and fines.** The M50 is barrier-free and must be paid by 8 p.m. the next day. Penalty points go to the driver, and you give the Gardaí the renter's details when asked.

**Northern Ireland.** Crossing the border needs your permission and cover.

**Fair terms.** Wording follows the [Consumer Rights Act 2022](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2022/act/37/enacted/en/html), and disputes can go to the European Car Rental Conciliation Service if you are a member, or Small Claims.

Every EU version also follows EU consumer law: prices shown with every compulsory charge included, damage charged only at its evidenced cost and never above the excess when a damage waiver applies, no keeping the whole deposit as a penalty, and the [14-day withdrawal right not applying to car rental for set dates](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj). Fines and renter details follow the GDPR, and the renter signs on paper or electronically, both valid under [eIDAS](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj).
