Southern Europe's islands and coasts are some of the busiest rental markets in the world. Visitors arrive by plane and ferry, need a car or scooter for a week, and book on their phones. Each country regulates rentals differently, though: Portugal and Greece have national procedures with fleet and vehicle-age rules, Italy works through the town hall, and Spain leaves most of it to general business law and the regions.

> This guide is general information, not legal advice. National rules change, and regions and municipalities add their own. Sources were checked on 8 November 2026. Confirm everything with the offices named below before you invest.

## What's the same everywhere

A few things apply across all four countries, because they come from EU law:

- **Third-party liability insurance** is compulsory for every vehicle, and EU guidance to travelers says cover valid in all EU countries must be included in the rental price ([Your Europe: hiring a car](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/driving-abroad/car-rental-abroad/index_en.htm), accessed 8 November 2026).
- **No 14-day cancellation right** for rentals booked online for specific dates, and **no different prices by nationality or residence**. Our guide to [EU rental agreements](/guides/rental-agreement-eu-consumer-law) covers these, plus card deposits and GDPR.
- **A registered business and a tax number,** VAT registration, and social security for you and any staff.

## Portugal: prior notice to IMT

Renting passenger vehicles without a driver ("rent-a-car") is governed by Decree-Law 181/2012 and its amendments. You don't get a license as such: you file a prior notice with the Institute for Mobility and Transport (IMT) through the government's digital portal, and the activity is open to individuals and companies established in Portugal. IMT lists these requirements ([IMT: rental of vehicles without a driver](https://www.imt-ip.pt/rodoviario/infraestruturas-rodoviarias/aluguer-veiculos-sem-condutor/), accessed 8 November 2026):

| Requirement | What IMT asks for |
|---|---|
| Minimum fleet | Seven light passenger cars, or three for motorcycles, mopeds, tricycles and quadricycles |
| Vehicle age | No more than five years from first registration |
| Premises | A fixed establishment for serving customers |
| Insurance | Civil liability and personal accident policy, with a copy submitted |
| Contracts | Standard-form contracts sent to the Mobility and Transport Authority (AMT) before you start |
| Other | Good standing of managers, tax and social security in order, 10% of the fleet meeting Euro 6 |

IMT says there's no fee for access. The five-year age limit matters for your plan: you'll replace vehicles more often than in Greece or Italy, so model resale values carefully.

## Italy: authorization through the town hall

Italy regulates rental without a driver through D.P.R. 481/2001 and Article 84 of the Highway Code. The Emilia-Romagna regional government's guidance summarizes how it works there ([Regione Emilia-Romagna: vehicle and boat rental](https://imprese.regione.emilia-romagna.it/semplificazione-e-sportello-unico/noleggio_di_veicoli_e_natanti_scheda_informativa.pdf), accessed 8 November 2026):

- Apply through the municipality's one-stop business office (SUAP).
- Vehicles must be owned by the business, or held on lease, and registered in its name for "locazione senza conducente" (rental without a driver), with the registration document issued by the regional Motorizzazione office.
- Register the business with the Companies Register and have a suitable garage or yard in the municipality.
- Keep a register of rentals, stamped by the mayor.
- The rules cover cars and also motorcycles, mopeds, scooters and bicycles.

Procedures and forms differ by region and town, so ask the SUAP where you'll operate for its checklist.

## Greece: notice to the Regional Tourism Office

In Greece, vehicle rental is a tourism business. You notify the Regional Tourism Office (Περιφερειακή Υπηρεσία Τουρισμού) of the area where you'll operate, under Law 4276/2014 and Presidential Decree 127/2017. The government's register of procedures sets out the requirements for cars and for motorcycles ([mitos.gov.gr: car rental](https://mitos.gov.gr/index.php/ΔΔ:Αναγγελία_Λειτουργίας_Επιχείρησης_Εκμίσθωσης_Επιβατηγών_Αυτοκινήτων_Ιδιωτικής_Χρήσης_%28Ι.Χ.%29); [mitos.gov.gr: motorcycle rental](https://mitos.gov.gr/index.php/ΔΔ:Αναγγελία_Λειτουργίας_Επιχείρησης_Εκμίσθωσης_Μοτοσικλετών,_Τρίτροχων_και_Τετράτροχων_άνω_των_50_κ.εκ._χωρίς_Οδηγό), both accessed 8 November 2026):

| | Cars | Motorcycles, tricycles, quads over 50 cc |
|---|---|---|
| Premises | An office; above 10 cars, also parking for at least half the fleet (6 m² per car) | An office; above 20 vehicles, also parking for at least half the fleet |
| Vehicle age | Up to 12 years from first registration | Up to 7 years (up to 123 cc) or 10 years (larger) |
| Extra kit | — | Helmets and eye protection for riders and passengers, to the official standard |
| Fee | €307.20 | €307.20 |

Greek rules also set specifications for the helmets you supply, so buy certified helmets in a range of sizes and replace any that have been dropped or involved in a crash.

## Spain: free to operate, with regional rules on top

Spain's national transport law (LOTT), as reworded in 2013, says rental of vehicles without a driver may be carried on freely by any business that meets its tax, social, labor and public or road safety obligations. It also allows regulations on the documents that prove a rental, its duration and the renter's right to use the vehicle ([Ministry of Transport: consolidated LOTT, September 2018 update](https://www.transportes.gob.es/recursos_mfom/paginabasica/recursos/8lott_2018-actualizacion_septiembre.pdf), accessed 8 November 2026).

So there's no national transport authorization, but you still need the usual business setup, vehicles registered and insured for rental, and whatever your autonomous community and town hall require for tourism businesses and premises. Islands in particular can have their own rules, so ask the regional tourism and transport departments before you commit.

## Seasons decide your numbers

Southern European rental demand is sharply seasonal: on islands and coasts it peaks in summer and drops off in winter. That changes how you plan:

- **Size the fleet for the shoulder season,** not August. Rent extra vehicles in peak months if you can, rather than owning bikes that sit idle all winter.
- **Price by season.** Set separate high-, mid- and low-season rates, and a longer-rental discount that still covers your costs. Our free [pricing calculator](/resources/pricing-calculator) checks each rate against your costs.
- **Find winter renters.** Monthly rentals to remote workers, seasonal staff and locals keep vehicles earning in the off-season. See our [long-term rental guide for Europe](/guides/long-term-car-rental-business-europe).
- **Plan vehicle age.** Portugal's five-year and Greece's seven-to-twelve-year limits set your replacement cycle.

## Deposits, agreements and handovers

Take a deposit sized to your insurance excess, and say exactly how it's held and when it's released: in the EU a card block needs the renter's consent to the exact amount. Use a clear, bilingual agreement (start from our free [rental agreement](/resources/rental-agreement)), and photograph every vehicle at handover and return using a [handover checklist](/guides/vehicle-handover-checklist). Most damage disputes are settled by photos.
