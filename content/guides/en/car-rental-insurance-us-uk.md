Insurance decides whether one bad rental is an annoyance or the end of a small business. The good news is that the building blocks are the same in the US and the UK: cover for harm you or your renters cause to other people, cover (or a plan) for damage to your own vehicles, and an agreement that says clearly who pays for what.

> This guide is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Talk to a licensed broker in your state or in the UK about your fleet. Sources were checked on 8 November 2026.

## The three layers of cover

Think of a rental car's risk in three layers:

1. **Liability to other people.** Injury to other road users, passengers and pedestrians, and damage to their property. This is the part the law makes compulsory.
2. **Damage to and theft of your vehicle.** Collision, vandalism, theft, glass and weather. Usually optional, but most lenders require it on financed cars.
3. **The gap between the two and the renter.** Your deductible (excess), loss of use while a car is off the road, and costs the renter causes, like tolls, fines and towing. Your deposit and your agreement handle this layer.

## United States

### Compulsory liability, set by each state

Every state sets its own minimum liability limits. California's are a useful example: from 1 January 2025 they are $30,000 per person and $60,000 per accident for injury or death, and $15,000 for property damage, up from $15,000/$30,000/$5,000 ([California Department of Insurance](https://www.insurance.ca.gov/0400-news/0102-alerts/2025/New-Year-Means-New-Changes-for-Insurance.cfm), accessed 8 November 2026). Your rental policy has to meet the minimums where your cars are registered. Many operators buy far more, because a serious injury claim can exceed state minimums many times over.

### A commercial policy that names rental use

A personal auto policy does not cover renting cars to the public. Ask a commercial broker for a policy written for rental, and check:

- **Who is covered to drive:** any renter on a signed agreement, or only named drivers? Is there a minimum age?
- **Whose policy pays first:** whether your cover pays before or after the renter's own auto insurance depends on state law and the policy wording. Ask your broker which applies where you operate, and say it in your agreement.
- **Exclusions:** rideshare and delivery use, driving out of state or into Mexico or Canada, unpaved roads, unlisted drivers.

### Vicarious liability

Federal law limits one risk. Under 49 U.S.C. 30106, often called the Graves Amendment, a business that rents or leases vehicles isn't liable under state law merely because it owns the vehicle, provided it wasn't negligent and didn't commit a crime ([49 U.S.C. 30106, govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/49/30106), accessed 8 November 2026). It does not cover your own negligence: renting a car with a known defect, skipping a license check or ignoring an open recall can still make you liable.

### Damage waivers are regulated in some states

A damage waiver (CDW or LDW) is the rental company agreeing not to charge the renter for damage, in exchange for a daily fee. Some states regulate them closely. California's Civil Code section 1939.09 requires the waiver to be optional, caps the daily fee for standard car classes at $25 adjusted each year for inflation since 2023, requires specific written and spoken disclosures, and lists the only situations where the waiver can be voided, such as reckless or drunk driving, unauthorized drivers or false information ([California Civil Code 1939.09](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1939.09), accessed 8 November 2026).

If you want to sell a waiver, check your own state's rules first. Many small operators skip waivers and use a clear deposit and deductible instead.

## United Kingdom

### Compulsory third-party cover

Section 143 of the Road Traffic Act 1988 makes it an offense to use a vehicle on a road or other public place without insurance covering that use, and also to cause or permit another person to use it without such cover ([Road Traffic Act 1988, s.143](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/52/section/143), accessed 8 November 2026). For a rental business, the second part matters: if you hand the keys to a renter your policy doesn't cover, you have committed an offense as well as the renter.

### Hire cover, not a private policy

UK insurers sell specific policies for vehicles hired out to the public, often called self-drive hire insurance. A private car policy won't cover it. When you get quotes, ask about minimum and maximum driver ages, years licensed, accepted licence types (including overseas licences), and whether the policy covers delivery and collection by your staff.

### Checking a renter's driving record

Drivers with a licence issued in England, Wales or Scotland can view their record and create a check code on GOV.UK to share with someone else. GOV.UK names a car hire company as an example of who they might share it with, and a code is valid for 21 days ([GOV.UK: view or share your driving licence information](https://www.gov.uk/view-driving-licence), accessed 8 November 2026). Ask for the code when the renter books, check the entitlement and penalty points before handover, and record that you did.

## Deductibles, deposits and the agreement

Whatever country you're in, the gap between what insurance pays and what a rental costs you is covered by three things you control:

- **A deductible you can afford.** A higher deductible lowers your premium, but you pay it every time.
- **A deposit sized to match.** Many operators set the deposit at or near the deductible, so a small claim comes out of the deposit and a large one goes to insurance. Our guide on [how much deposit to charge](/guides/rental-deposit-policy) covers sizes, payment methods and returns.
- **An agreement that says who pays.** List what the renter is responsible for (damage up to the deductible, fuel, tolls, parking and speeding tickets, towing, lost keys), what voids cover (unlisted drivers, drink driving, off-road use), and how you'll show the damage (handover photos).
