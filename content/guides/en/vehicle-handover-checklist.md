*By Anan · Updated 28 October 2026*

Most damage disputes are not really about the damage. They are about whether it was there before. If you can show a clear, dated photo of the same panel at handover and at return, the conversation usually ends in a minute. If you cannot, it becomes your word against the renter's, and the renter writes the review.

This guide gives you a handover routine you can do in five minutes, for scooters and cars.

> This guide is general information, not legal advice.

## The 12-photo protocol

Take the same 12 photos, in the same order, at every handover and every return. Consistency matters: it makes comparison quick, and it shows you treat every renter the same.

1. **Front, straight on,** with the number plate readable.
2. **Front left corner,** at about 45 degrees.
3. **Left side,** the whole vehicle in frame.
4. **Rear left corner,** at about 45 degrees.
5. **Rear, straight on,** with the number plate readable.
6. **Rear right corner,** at about 45 degrees.
7. **Right side,** the whole vehicle in frame.
8. **Front right corner,** at about 45 degrees.
9. **Dashboard** with the ignition on, showing the odometer and fuel gauge clearly.
10. **Wheels and tyres:** both wheels on a scooter, all four on a car (a quick burst is fine).
11. **Seat and interior:** scooter seat and under-seat box; car front seats, rear seats, floor and boot.
12. **Accessories laid out together:** keys, helmets, phone holder, rain poncho, registration copy, child seat or anything else you hand over.

Then add **one close-up for every existing mark**: scratches, dents, cracks, worn seat covers. Hold a finger or a coin near the mark to show its size.

Tips for useful photos:

- Shoot in daylight or good light. Avoid photographing a wet vehicle, because water hides scratches.
- Stand far enough back to show the whole panel, then go close for marks.
- Keep the plate in at least two photos so nobody can claim it was a different vehicle.

## Timestamps and metadata

A photo is strongest evidence when it is clear when it was taken. Most phone cameras save the date, time and often the location inside the photo file. Check your phone's clock is set automatically.

The catch: many chat apps compress photos and strip that metadata when you send them ([Fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/whatsapp-telegram-signal-photo-metadata-preservation/)). So:

- **Keep the originals.** Do not delete them from your phone after sending them to the renter.
- **Back them up** to cloud storage or a rental system that stores the time each photo was taken.
- **Send copies to the renter** in chat right after handover. Even without metadata, the chat itself shows when they were sent, and the renter has seen them.
- **Name or file by booking.** "Booking 1043, handover, 12 May" is much easier to find than photo 4,812 in your camera roll.

## Fuel and odometer

Photo 9 does two jobs. It records how much fuel was in the tank and the starting distance.

- **Fuel:** write the level on the agreement too (for example, "half" or "4 of 8 bars"). Agree the return rule: same level, or a refuelling charge per bar.
- **Odometer:** write the reading on the agreement. If you have a distance limit (common on monthly rentals), the return photo shows whether it was exceeded.
- **Warning lights:** if any light is on at handover, note it. You do not want to be blamed for a fault later, and the renter does not want to be blamed for one either.

## Accessories

Small items cause a surprising number of arguments. Photo 12 is your record of what went out. Count everything back in at return, and list the replacement price of each item on your damage price list. Common items:

- Keys and key fob (and spare, if given)
- Helmets, with size noted
- Phone holder and charging cable
- Rain poncho, seat cover
- Registration copy and insurance card
- Child seat, roof rack, cool box, beach equipment

## Walking round with the renter

Do the walk-round together. It takes a few minutes and prevents most disputes.

1. **Start at the front and walk clockwise,** taking the photos in order.
2. **Point out each existing mark** and let the renter look. Ask "anything else you can see?"
3. **Show the dashboard** and agree fuel and odometer.
4. **Check the controls:** brakes, lights, indicators, horn. On a car, also air conditioning, wipers and the spare wheel.
5. **Explain the return rules:** time, place, fuel, what happens if they are late or have an accident.
6. **Ask the renter to sign** the agreement, which refers to the photos.

## Add a short video, and handle deliveries properly

Photos are your main record, but a 30 to 60 second video walking slowly round the vehicle is a useful backup. It shows the vehicle as a whole, with no gaps between angles, and it is hard to argue that a video was staged. Start the video on the number plate and end on the dashboard.

If you deliver vehicles to hotels or villas, the handover often happens in a car park with a renter in a hurry. Keep the same routine:

- **Take the full set of photos before you leave the shop,** in good light, so you have a clean "out" record even if the delivery handover is rushed.
- **Repeat the walk-round with the renter at delivery.** If they will not wait, send the photos in chat straight away and ask them to reply within an hour if they see anything you missed.
- **For collections,** take the return photos where you collect, before you ride or drive the vehicle back. Any mark that appears after you have moved it is hard to put on the renter.

## Scooters vs cars

The protocol is the same, but the problem areas differ.

| | Scooters | Cars |
|---|---|---|
| Where damage hides | Under the body panels, brake and clutch levers, mirrors, footboard, exhaust | Bumper corners, alloy wheels, windscreen chips, underside, door edges |
| Common small losses | Helmets, keys, phone holders | Keys, fuel cap, parcel shelf, floor mats |
| Fall damage | Scratches on both sides at the widest points; bent levers and mirrors | Rarely falls; look for kerb rash on wheels |
| Extra photos | Under the seat; ends of handlebars | Boot with spare wheel and tools; windscreen close-up |
| Typical time | 3–4 minutes | 5–8 minutes |

For scooters, look carefully at the ends of the handlebars, the mirrors and the lower edges of the side panels. These touch the ground first when a bike drops. Fresh scratches in these spots usually mean a fall, even if the renter says nothing.

## When you find damage at return

Even with a perfect routine, you will find new damage sometimes. Handle it the same way every time:

1. **Stay calm and do the full return photos first.** Do not start with the argument.
2. **Photograph the damage** from the same angle as the matching handover photo, then close up.
3. **Show the renter both photos** side by side on your phone.
4. **Use your damage price list** for listed items. For anything else, explain that you will get a mechanic's quote and when you will send it.
5. **Agree the amount in writing,** in chat or on the return form, and refund the rest of the deposit.
6. **If the renter disputes it,** listen. If the handover photo does not clearly show the panel, be fair and consider splitting the cost. Our guide on [deposits and damage disputes](/guides/rental-deposit-policy) covers deduction rules and how to respond to bad reviews.

If the damage is serious (an accident, injury or theft), follow your insurer's process and file a police report. Record the renter's statement in writing.

## Storing evidence

Keep handover and return photos for as long as a claim could reasonably come up, such as late traffic fines, insurance claims or a renter's card chargeback. A common practice is to keep them at least several months after the rental ends. If your country has data protection rules, keep only what you need and store it securely.

A simple folder structure works for a few vehicles: one folder per booking, with the agreement, payment slip and photos inside. Once you have more vehicles, it becomes hard to find the right photo quickly.
