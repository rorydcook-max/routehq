> Updated 28 October 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

Monthly rentals are a different business from daily tourist rentals. Fewer handovers, steadier income, quieter vehicles. But the money is tied up for longer, problems take longer to show, and a bad renter can cost you a month rather than a day.

This guide covers how to price, screen, collect and maintain when your customers are expats, digital nomads, retirees and long-stay visitors who want a car or scooter for one to twelve months.

## Who rents monthly, and what they want

Long-stay customers are not tourists on a three-day trip. Typical monthly renters include:

- **Digital nomads and remote workers** staying one to six months, often on a long-stay visa.
- **Retirees and snowbirds** who come back for the same season every year.
- **Expats between cars**, waiting for a purchase, a work permit or a move.
- **Thai and foreign workers** on fixed-term contracts at hotels, schools or construction sites.

They care about reliability, a fair monthly price, being able to reach you when something breaks, and not being stung on the deposit at the end. They care less about having the newest model. A well-maintained three-year-old Honda Click or Toyota Yaris is exactly what many of them want.

## Build a pricing ladder

Price so the longer the rental, the lower the daily equivalent, but never so low that a month barely covers your costs.

**Worked example (illustrative only, not market rates):**

| | Daily | Weekly | Monthly | 3 months+ (per month) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 125cc scooter | ฿250 | ฿1,400 (฿200/day) | ฿3,500 (฿117/day) | ฿3,000 (฿100/day) |
| Small car (e.g. Yaris) | ฿1,000 | ฿6,000 (฿857/day) | ฿15,000 (฿500/day) | ฿13,500 (฿450/day) |

In this example a month costs 14 times the daily rate for a scooter and 15 times for a car. That can still be the better deal for you, because:

- a daily-rental scooter at ฿250 that is rented 18 days a month earns ฿4,500 before cleaning, handover time, booking fees and wear from many different riders;
- the monthly scooter earns ฿3,500 with one handover, no empty days and one rider.

Run the numbers on your own occupancy. If your daily fleet sits at 85% in high season, a cheap monthly deal in December loses money. If it sits at 40% in low season, the same deal is a win. The [pricing calculator](/resources/pricing-calculator) does this comparison for you.

Price adjustments that work well for long rentals:

- **Season-aware monthly rates.** Charge more for months that start in high season, and offer your best rate for rentals that start in low season.
- **Commitment discounts.** A lower rate for three or six months paid monthly, but with the early-return rules (below) written in.
- **Mileage cap for cars**, for example 2,500 km a month included and a per-km charge above that. It protects you from someone using the car for deliveries.

## Screen renters properly

One bad monthly renter can cost more than a season of good ones. A short, consistent check works better than gut feeling.

- **ID and licence.** Passport, visa or entry stamp, and a valid licence for the vehicle class. Under the standard contract forms, a foreign renter shows an international driving permit under a convention Thailand is party to, or a Thai driving licence. Check the motorbike category specifically: many visitors only hold a car licence.
- **Address in Thailand.** Where they are staying, ideally with a lease or booking you can see.
- **Contact details** on at least two channels, such as phone plus LINE or WhatsApp.
- **History.** Returning customers and referrals are your best renters. For new ones, a short call or video chat tells you a lot.
- **Purpose.** Ask what they will use the vehicle for. Delivery riding, ride-hailing and sub-renting are common reasons for heavy wear, and the standard contract forbids sub-letting or lending the vehicle without your written permission.

## Deposits for long rentals

A deposit for a month-long rental should reflect what can go wrong in a month, not in a day.

- **Size it from your real exposure.** A common approach is the insurance excess plus the cost of a typical repair, often landing near one month's rent. For a scooter with only compulsory insurance, think about the replacement cost if it is stolen.
- **Take it by bank transfer** rather than holding a passport. Holding passports as security is widely discouraged in Thailand and creates problems if the renter needs it for immigration.
- **Write the deduction rules down.** Since 30 December 2025, consumer vehicle rentals are a [contract-controlled business](https://www.ocpb.go.th/news_view_en.php?nid=17127). The official forms say you must inspect the vehicle on return, raise any damage at that moment, and claim only the actual cost with evidence. A clause that lets you keep the deposit when the renter has not breached the contract is banned.
- **Return it on time.** The forms published with the notice set short deadlines: where nothing is damaged, a cash or transfer deposit goes back on the day you receive the vehicle, with a longer window for refunds to a credit card. Check the timings in the [published notice](https://www.takhamcity.go.th/news/detail/136343) and build them into your process.

Photos at handover and return are what make deductions defensible. Use the [handover checklist](/resources/handover-checklist) every time, including for repeat renters.

## Monthly payments and reminders

Most long-stay customers prefer to pay month by month. That's fine, as long as the rules are clear.

- **Rent in advance.** Each month's rent is due before the month starts, not at the end.
- **A fixed due date** that matches the rental start date, for example the 5th of every month.
- **Reminders before, on and after the due date.** Three days before, on the day, and one day late. Keep the tone friendly; most late payments are forgetfulness.
- **A late-payment rule in the contract**, such as a grace period of two days, then a daily fee, and the right to collect the vehicle after a stated number of days unpaid. Be careful that the rule is proportionate and written into the agreement before the rental starts.
- **Receipts for every payment**, showing the period covered.

## Maintenance during a rental: who pays what

A car rented for six months will need servicing during the rental. Agree who does what before it happens.

| Item | Usually paid by | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduled service (oil, filters) | Operator | Ask the renter to bring it in at a set mileage or date; you book and pay |
| Tyres, brakes, battery from normal wear | Operator | Inspect at each service |
| Breakdown from mechanical failure | Operator | Plus a replacement vehicle if it's off the road for more than a day |
| Fuel, tolls, parking | Renter | |
| Cleaning during the rental | Renter | Set a return-condition standard |
| Damage caused by the renter | Renter, up to the agreed limit | Claim actual cost with evidence |
| Traffic and parking fines | Renter | The forms let you recover fines the renter caused, at actual cost |
| Punctures | Agree in advance | Many operators split: renter pays repairs, operator replaces worn tyres |

Two rules from the standard contract forms matter here:

- **Replacement vehicle.** If the rented car or bike can't be used for more than one day because of an accident or maintenance, you must provide the same or a similar model until it is fixed, unless the renter caused the problem by breaking the law.
- **Your obligations don't pause.** The forms make the operator responsible for keeping annual tax paid and insurance in force for the whole rental period.

Practical tips:

- **Schedule services by date as well as mileage.** For scooters, an oil change every 1,000 to 2,000 km or two to three months is typical. Check your manufacturer's schedule.
- **Make servicing easy for the renter.** Offer to collect the vehicle, or swap it for a spare for the morning.
- **Keep a log per vehicle**: date, mileage, work done, cost. It helps with resale and with damage disputes.

## Tax sticker, พ.ร.บ. and insurance renewals inside a rental

On a twelve-month rental, every annual renewal falls inside it. Plan for them.

- **Annual tax (ป้ายวงกลม).** Private vehicles can renew up to 90 days early, and vehicles over a certain age need an inspection at a private inspection centre (ตรอ.) first: cars from seven years and motorbikes from five years after first registration ([KTC guide](https://www.ktc.co.th/article/knowledge/vehicle-tax-renewal-document-and-age-atpb)). Late tax adds a surcharge of 1% a month. Green-plate rental cars can only renew 30 days early and must be inspected first ([DLT service guide](https://infocenter.oic.go.th/FILEWEB/CABINFOCENTER4/DRAWER032/GENERAL/DATA0000/00000142.PDF)).
- **Compulsory insurance (พ.ร.บ.).** You need a valid พ.ร.บ. to renew tax. From 1 September 2026 the regulator allows policy terms shorter than a year or up to two years, priced pro rata ([Howden summary of the new rules](https://www.howdengroup.com/th-th/thai-compulsory-motor-insurance)), but if you use it for tax, it must cover at least until the tax expires the following year.
- **Voluntary insurance.** Renew before it lapses, and make sure the renewal still says the vehicle is rented. More in our [insurance guide](/guides/rental-vehicle-insurance-thailand).

The simplest method: renew everything a month before it is due, and swap the renter into another vehicle for half a day if the car has to go for inspection. Put the new tax sticker and insurance documents in the vehicle and send the renter a photo.

## Swapping vehicles mid-rental

Swaps happen for services, breakdowns, accidents, a renter wanting a bigger car, or you needing a specific vehicle back for a high-season booking. Treat every swap like a new handover:

- Record the outgoing vehicle's condition, mileage and fuel, and the incoming one's.
- Update the contract or add a signed annex listing the new registration number. The standard form names the vehicle by registration number, engine and chassis number.
- Agree whether the rate changes if the replacement is a different class.
- Never swap into a vehicle with expired tax or insurance, even for a day.

## Low season

Long rentals are your best tool for low season. Some ideas:

- **Sell low-season months early.** Offer returning customers a rate for May to October before they leave in April.
- **Target workers, not tourists.** Hotel staff, teachers and contractors rent through low season.
- **Lower monthly rates for longer commitments**, but keep the early-return terms honest.
- **Use quiet months for maintenance**, repaints and replacing tired vehicles, rather than discounting everything.

## Cash flow: monthly vs daily

Monthly rentals smooth your income. Daily rentals spike it. Most operators do better with a mix.

| | Daily rental | Monthly rental |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue per vehicle per day | Higher | Lower |
| Occupancy | Seasonal, often 40–90% | Close to 100% while rented |
| Handovers, cleaning, admin | Many | Few |
| Wear and damage risk | Many riders, short use | One renter, long use |
| Cash flow | Lumpy, high season heavy | Predictable monthly |
| Marketing effort | Constant | Lower, with repeat customers |

A practical split for a small fleet is to keep a core of vehicles on monthly rentals to cover fixed costs, like loan repayments and insurance, and keep the rest for daily rentals in high season.

## Contract clauses specific to monthly rentals

Start from a contract that follows the official form, then make sure these points are covered. Our [free bilingual rental agreement](/resources/rental-agreement) includes them.

1. **Rental period and renewal.** Start and end dates and times, and how to extend. Agree that an extension is a written update, not just a message.
2. **Payment schedule.** Amount per month, due date, payment method, late fee and grace period.
3. **Early return by the renter.** The standard car form lets a renter on a monthly or yearly rental return the car early with advance notice. The published form text reads "ภายในสามสิบวัน" (within 30 days). You then refund unused rent pro rata and return the deposit under the normal rules. Price your commitment discounts with this in mind.
4. **Termination by you.** You can only end the contract early if the renter breaches it or breaks an essential condition. A clause letting you cancel without cause is banned. List what counts as a breach: unpaid rent past the grace period, sub-letting, ride-hailing or delivery work, taking the vehicle out of Thailand, or drink-driving.
5. **Servicing.** How and when the renter must make the vehicle available, and that you'll provide a replacement.
6. **Mileage limits** (for cars) and the excess charge.
7. **Who may drive.** Named drivers only, matching your insurance.
8. **Area of use.** For example, not off the island without permission, and never outside Thailand (the standard form already bans taking the vehicle abroad).
9. **Fines.** Renter pays fines incurred during the rental, at actual cost with evidence.
10. **Return condition and fuel.** The form expects the vehicle back with the fuel level it left with; you can charge only the actual cost of any shortfall.
11. **Late return.** A stated hourly and daily charge, which the form allows you to take from the deposit.

Write the contract in Thai, as the rules require, and give the renter an English version alongside. Give them a signed copy immediately.

## A simple monthly workflow

- **Before the rental**: screen, sign the agreement, take the deposit and first month's rent, record the handover.
- **Every month**: reminder, payment, receipt, quick check-in message.
- **Every service interval**: book, swap if needed, log.
- **30 days before any renewal**: tax, พ.ร.บ., insurance.
- **At the end**: inspection with photos, deposit back on time, ask for a review or a booking for next season.
